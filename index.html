What is Rakshabandhan?

🪢

The Sacred Thread

Raksha Bandhan, meaning "the knot of protection," is a Hindu festival celebrating the eternal bond between brothers and sisters. Sisters tie a sacred thread — the Rakhi — on their brother's wrist as a symbol of love and protection.

📅

When Is It?

Celebrated on the full moon day (Purnima) of the Hindu month of Shravana, typically falling in August. The day begins with morning prayers and culminates in joyous family celebrations.

🪔

Beyond Siblings

The festival transcends bloodlines — cousins, friends, and even citizens tie rakhis to express bonds of care and mutual protection. It is a celebration of every relationship anchored in unconditional love.

The Sacred Ritual

🛁

Preparation and Bath

Both siblings bathe and wear new or traditional clothes. The sister prepares the Puja thali with all essentials.

🪔

Puja Thali

The thali holds Rakhi, roli, chawal, diya, sweets, and fruits — each item holding deep symbolic meaning.

🌊

Aarti and Tilak

The sister performs aarti and applies a tilak of roli and chawal on the brother's forehead, praying for his long life.

🪢

Tying the Rakhi

The sacred Rakhi is tied around the brother's right wrist while prayers are chanted — the centrepiece of the festival.

🍬

Exchange of Sweets

Sweets are exchanged between siblings. The brother gives a gift as a token of love and promise of protection.

🎁

Gifts and Blessings

The celebration continues with family feasts, exchange of gifts, and heartfelt blessings.
